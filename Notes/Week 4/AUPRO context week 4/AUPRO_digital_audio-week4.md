## Sound Power Level (SWL)
* Sound power level is a measure of the total power radiated in all directions by a sound source. It is sometimes referred to as PWL.
* Useful for comparing the total acoustic power radiated by a sound source. Sound power has no particular direction.
* Sound power is measured in watts.
* Most everyday sound sources generate a fraction of a watt of sound power. 20 watts of sound power would be close to our pain threshold. Not to be confused with the power output of an amplifier; loudspeakers are only about 1% efficient, meaning that a 100 watt amplifier only generates 1 watt of sound power!

## Sound Pressure Level (SPL)
* The effect of sound power on its surroundings. SPL is measured in newtons per square metre ($N m^{-2}$).
* Human hearing is sensitive to pressure, so pressure is used as a measure of the amplitude of a sound wave.
* Sound pressure is the RMS pressure of a sound wave at a particular point. Pressure is measured in Pascals.
* 0 dB SPL is approximately equivalent to the threshold of hearing ($2 \times 10^{-5} Nm^{-2} or 20 \mu Pa) at 1 kHz. The threshold of pain is held as 20 Pa at 1 kHz. Real sounds vary over a range of pressure amplitudes greater than 1 million.

## Useful analogy
Here's an analogy for the difference between sound power and sound pressure. If sound power was the heat energy generated by a radiator, then sound pressure would be the temperature of the air in the room. People entering the room would feel the temperature, but the radiator is the source of power.

## Clock accuracy

Digital audio devices rely on precise timing to convert sound between analogue and digital formats. This timing is governed by a **clock**, which ensures samples are taken and played back at exactly the right intervals.

- **Clocking** prevents timing errors between devices, which can cause pops, clicks, or degraded audio quality.
- **Word clock** sends a regular electrical pulse (e.g. 48,000 times per second at 48kHz) to synchronise devices.
- **Jitter** is the deviation in clock timing; higher jitter leads to phase distortion and loss of detail, especially in high frequencies.
- When using multiple devices, a **master clock** is needed to keep everything in sync.

See <https://www.sweetwater.com/sweetcare/articles/understanding-digital-clocking-for-audio/> for some more info.


## Loading the buffer in SC

### Full file path
```js
p = Buffer.read
(s, "/Users/yourname/Desktop/subfolder/filename.wav");
```

### Relative file path
```js
p = Buffer.read
(s, thisProcess.nowExecutingPath.dirname 
+/+ "filename.wav");
```

## Playbuf

```js
(
{

	PlayBuf.ar(
		2, // number of channels - in this case, stereo
		p.bufnum, // which buffer to play
		BufRateScale.kr(p.bufnum), // play at the recorded rate
		loop: 1 // loop the file
	)
}.play
)
```

## Using an LLM for project ideation

Consider using an LLM to help generate and refine ideas for your technical artefact.

1. **Start with the brief**: Copy the assessment description or learning outcomes into the LLM (e.g. 'What kinds of audio software projects could meet these criteria?').
2. **Add your interests or inspirations**: Mention genres, artists, tools, or techniques you’re drawn to (e.g. 'How could I design a tool inspired by [X]?').
3. **Include technical constraints**: Specify that you’ll be using **SuperCollider** and emphasise the deadline to set the timeframe (e.g. 'What’s a manageable project scope for this context?').
4. **Refine your idea**: Share a rough concept and ask for feature suggestions, DSP/synthesis techniques, and GUI or control options (e.g. 'How could I implement this in SuperCollider?').
